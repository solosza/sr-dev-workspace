## 0. THE HEADLINE: Nevada AB 396 (the "Casita Law")

**What the owner is referring to is a *state* law, not a single city/county ordinance.** It is the forcing mechanism behind every jurisdiction's 2025–2026 ADU code rewrite below.

- **Bill:** Nevada Assembly Bill 396 (2025 Legislative Session, 83rd Session), sponsored by Assemblymember Shea Backus. Second Reprint text: [AB396_R2.pdf](https://archive.leg.state.nv.us/Session/83rd2025/Bills/AB/AB396_R2.pdf) (archive.leg.state.nv.us).
- **Effective/compliance deadline:** **July 1, 2026.**
- **Who it covers:** Every county with population ≥ 100,000 (Clark and Washoe) and every city with population ≥ 60,000 (Las Vegas, Henderson, North Las Vegas, Reno, Sparks) — i.e., **all four Las Vegas–valley governing bodies relevant to this research** — must adopt an ordinance authorizing ADU development on residential property by the deadline.
- **What it requires ordinances to allow, at minimum:** a full accessory dwelling unit with its own kitchen and bathroom (jurisdictions may no longer categorically ban second kitchens); no more than **1 additional off-street parking space** may be required; HOAs may not prohibit ADUs outright (architectural review of design/materials is still allowed).
- **Non-compliance consequence:** if a covered city/county does NOT adopt a compliant ordinance by July 1, 2026, **ADUs become authorized by state law on any residentially zoned parcel in that jurisdiction without further local restriction.** This is the backstop that made the deadline real leverage — every valley jurisdiction moved to adopt its own ordinance rather than default to the state's blanket authorization.
- **Status as of this research date (2026-07-22):** deadline has passed; all four jurisdictions below report compliant action taken (three by amendment, one — North Las Vegas — by pre-existing-compliance determination). Codified text of the newest amendments (Las Vegas's Ordinance 6963) was reported as "not yet published" by secondary sources as of the ordinance's July 1, 2026 adoption date — **flag as PROPOSED-TEXT-PENDING-CODIFICATION for LVMC 19.06/19.12/19.18 specifically; re-verify against the live Las Vegas Municipal Code before relying on exact dimensional figures.**

Sources: [Fox5 Vegas, "New Nevada law expands options for ADUs, 'casitas' as cities update codes by July 1" (2026-06-19)](https://www.fox5vegas.com/2026/06/19/nevada-casita-law-adds-more-housing-options-across-las-vegas/); [Nevada News & Views, "Nevada's 'Casita' Law"](https://nevadanewsandviews.com/nevadas-casita-law-more-housing-options-less-government-red-tape/); [K&Co, "Nevada AB 396 Casita Law: A Las Vegas Homeowner Guide"](https://kingdomandco.com/nevada-ab-396-casita-law/); [TrackBill AB396 tracker](https://trackbill.com/bill/nevada-assembly-bill-396-revises-provisions-relating-to-housing-bdr-22-232/2692323/).

**Nevada state-law definition of "accessory dwelling unit":** a structure on the property of, or as an addition to, a primary residence, with its own kitchen and bathroom. This is distinct from Clark County's own code term **"casita,"** defined locally as a separate structure *without* a kitchen (see §2 below) — a casita in Clark County usage is NOT the same thing as an AB 396 "ADU"; it's a lesser-regulated category. Confirm which term applies to the owner's intended structure before using either term with a planning counter.

## 2. City of Las Vegas (LVMC Title 19)

- **Governing ordinance for this rewrite:** **Ordinance No. 6963 (Bill 2026-24)**, adopted by Las Vegas City Council **July 1, 2026**, amending **LVMC 19.06, 19.12, and 19.18** to bring the city into AB 396 compliance. Introduced ~June 17, 2026; adopted on the statutory deadline itself. **Codified text pending publication as of this research** — the figures below are as reported by secondary sources tracking the ordinance; re-verify against the live LVMC before design.
- **What it changed (the headline effect):** removed the prior **6,500 sq ft minimum-lot** threshold that previously triggered a discretionary **Special Use Permit (SUP)** hearing; a conforming ADU — including one with a full second kitchen — can now go through as a **standard ministerial building permit**, no public hearing. Removed the prior one-bedroom restriction on ADUs.
- **Permitted zones:** single-family residential zoning districts (pre-2026 use tables referenced U/R-A/R-E/R-D/R-1; confirm updated LVMC 19.12 use table reflects the same list post-Ordinance 6963).
- **Lot size minimum:** removed (was 6,500 sq ft; no longer a threshold for SUP).
- **Max ADU size:** attached ADU up to 50% of the primary dwelling's floor area; both attached and detached capped at **1,200 sq ft**.
- **Attached / detached / garage conversion:** both attached and detached ADUs permitted; garage conversions occur but city-specific procedural treatment for conversions was not confirmed in this pass — verify with Planning. Typical conversion cost cited in market sources: $60,000–$140,000 (cost estimate, not a code figure).
- **Setbacks (pre-2026 baseline, re-verify post-Ordinance-6963):** rear — 3 ft minimum for detached accessory structures plus a 6 ft separation from the main dwelling; side — same 3 ft / 6 ft-separation standard, with corner lots matching the main dwelling's street-side setback; front — detached ADUs prohibited in the front setback.
- **Height (pre-2026 baseline, re-verify):** 2 stories / 35 ft, or the main building's height, whichever is less.
- **Coverage:** not confirmed in this pass — verify with LVMC 19.06/19.12 lot-coverage tables for the applicable zone.
- **Parking:** capped at the AB 396 statewide maximum of **1 additional off-street space.**
- **Owner-occupancy:** pre-2026 code required owner-occupancy of the primary residence to rent the ADU; **treatment under Ordinance 6963 is unconfirmed — verify directly with Las Vegas Planning before assuming either way.**
- **Short-term-rental restriction:** ADU rental agreements must be for a term **exceeding 30 days**; short nightly-rental use requires separate STR licensing (which the city restricts heavily) — an ADU is not automatically STR-eligible.
- **Utility/sewer:** standard city utility connection process; no ADU-specific carve-out identified in this pass.
- **# ADUs per lot:** **1 per property.**
- **Permit path / fees / timeline:** conforming ADUs (including full kitchens) bypass the former SUP process (which carried a **~$1,030 SUP fee** and a public-hearing timeline of several months) and go through as a standard building permit with **valuation-based fees**. Overall timeline not independently confirmed post-Ordinance-6963 — confirm current over-the-counter vs. plan-review timeline with Las Vegas Planning.
- **Ministerial vs. discretionary:** **ministerial** for conforming ADUs as of Ordinance 6963 — this is the central procedural change from the prior discretionary-SUP regime.

Sources: [aduzoning.org, "ADU Rules in Las Vegas, NV (2026)"](https://www.aduzoning.org/adu-rules/las-vegas-nv/); [Fox5 Vegas (2026-06-19)](https://www.fox5vegas.com/2026/06/19/nevada-casita-law-adds-more-housing-options-across-las-vegas/); [Steadily, "ADU Housing Laws and Regulations in Las Vegas - 2026"](https://www.steadily.com/blog/adu-laws-regulations-las-vegas); [K&Co AB396 guide](https://kingdomandco.com/nevada-ab-396-casita-law/).

## 3. Unincorporated Clark County (Title 30)

- **Governing chapters:** Title 30 Unified Development Code, specifically **Chapter 30.44 (Uses)** for the accessory-apartment/casita/guest-quarters standards and **Chapter 30.08 (Definitions)** for the term definitions. Title 30 was originally adopted June 21, 2000, amended repeatedly since; full text: [title30.pdf](https://www.clarkcountynv.gov/assets/documents/government/departments/comprehensive_planning_department/library/title30.pdf), [Chapter 30.44 PDF](https://www.clarkcountynv.gov/adobe/assets/urn:aaid:aem:302f8dbd-3100-4535-b513-df3f4d7a75fb/original/as/3044.pdf), [Chapter 30.08 PDF](https://www.clarkcountynv.gov/adobe/assets/urn:aaid:aem:7f939617-e55b-4ec2-9779-37f0f74ed6dc/original/as/3008.pdf). **These PDFs could not be full-text-extracted in this research pass (encoded/scanned format) — the section numbers are confirmed to exist and hold the relevant standards, but the owner or a follow-up pass should pull exact dimensional figures directly from them or via the Clark County planning counter.**
- **Casita vs. accessory apartment — county-specific definitions (Ch. 30.08):**
  - **"Casita"** = a separate structure on a lot with a dwelling, **without a kitchen**, used residentially (lower regulatory bar).
  - **"Accessory apartment"** = includes cooking facilities (kitchen/kitchenette) but is *not* counted as an additional "dwelling unit" for density-per-acre calculation purposes.
  - Related defined terms in the same chapter: "guest quarters," "temporary living quarters."
- **What changed (the headline effect):** code amendment effective **March 5, 2026** (Board of Commissioners vote) removed prior **lot-size limitations** on "accessory living quarters" county-wide. **Specific ordinance number for this March 5, 2026 amendment was not located in this research pass — flag for the owner to confirm the exact ordinance number with Clark County Comprehensive Planning or the County Clerk's [county-code page](https://www.clarkcountynv.gov/government/elected_officials/county_clerk/county-code) if the ordinance number is needed for a permit citation.**
- **Special Use Permit trigger post-amendment:** SUP still required for lots **under 4,000 sq ft**, or for any property within a **mobile-home or tiny-home park**. Lots at/above 4,000 sq ft no longer need the SUP for this reason.
- **Permitted zones:** single-family residential zones and compatible special-use zones per the Unified Development Code; guest casitas (no kitchen) generally permitted with setback/coverage compliance across unincorporated single-family zones; full accessory apartments (with kitchen) face stricter review and rental restrictions in some zones. **Zone-by-zone table not independently confirmed — verify against Ch. 30.44's use tables for the applicable zoning district.**
- **# per lot:** **1 accessory apartment, casita, guest quarters, OR temporary living quarters per lot** (these categories share a single-per-lot cap — cannot stack multiple types on one parcel). Recreational vehicles are explicitly NOT permitted as any of these categories.
- **Lot size minimum:** removed as of March 5, 2026 (see above), except the 4,000 sq ft SUP threshold.
- **Max size / setbacks / height / coverage:** not independently confirmed from the primary PDF in this pass (extraction failure) — the owner must pull exact figures from Ch. 30.44 directly or via the planning counter before design. Do not assume the City of Las Vegas figures (1,200 sq ft cap, etc.) apply — county standards may differ.
- **Parking:** subject to the AB 396 statewide 1-space cap.
- **Owner-occupancy:** not confirmed in this pass for the county specifically — verify.
- **Short-term-rental restriction:** three separate approval layers apply for any rental use — (1) zoning (may restrict independent-household rental of a casita/accessory apartment), (2) STR licensing (most nightly-rental applications fail county/city STR licensing for these structures), (3) HOA CC&Rs. Furnished rentals of 30+ days are the practical path where zoning and CC&Rs allow it.
- **Utility/sewer:** trenching, water, sewer, and electrical extension typically required for a full accessory apartment. **Sewer lateral connection can run $8,000–$20,000** depending on distance to the main and jurisdictional fees. Mini-split HVAC is typically required rather than ducting off the main home's system.
- **Permit path / fees / timeline:** zoning verification (confirm parcel's zone and any SUP trigger) → HOA architectural approval if applicable → design/engineering → permit submittal (**4–10 weeks** review) → construction with inspections → certificate of completion. **Total realistic timeline: 7–12 months.** Permit fees typically **$2,000–$5,000 all-in** (building permit; does not include utility connection fees noted above).
- **Ministerial vs. discretionary:** ministerial (standard permit, no hearing) for lots ≥4,000 sq ft outside mobile/tiny-home parks as of the March 5, 2026 amendment; **discretionary (SUP, public hearing)** for lots under 4,000 sq ft or within mobile/tiny-home parks.

Sources: [Fox5 Vegas (2026-06-19)](https://www.fox5vegas.com/2026/06/19/nevada-casita-law-adds-more-housing-options-across-las-vegas/); [Nevada Real Estate Group, "Building an ADU or Casita in Clark County: 2026 Rules"](https://www.nevadarealestategroup.com/blog/building-adu-casita-clark-county-rules-2026/); Clark County Code Title 30 Chapters 30.08 and 30.44 (PDFs above — section existence confirmed via site search, full text not extracted this pass).

## 4. City of Henderson (Development Code)

- **Status relative to AB 396:** Henderson made **substantial ADU expansions in 2022**, following American Planning Association recommendations — well ahead of AB 396. **Minor amendments passed fall 2025** to bring the existing framework into full AB 396 compliance (specific ordinance number/date not located in this pass — verify with Henderson Development Services).
- **Governing code:** [Henderson Development Code](https://www.cityofhenderson.com/government/development-code), Part II (District Regulations) and Part III (Citywide Standards), online at [online.encodeplus.com/regs/henderson-nv](https://online.encodeplus.com/regs/henderson-nv/doc-viewer.aspx?tocid=001.003).
- **Permitted zones:** residential zoning districts; specific standards vary by district — pre-application consultation with Development Services recommended, as smaller infill lots may not qualify without a variance.
- **Max ADU size:** detached — capped at **1,200 sq ft**; attached — lesser of 50% of primary dwelling floor area or 1,200 sq ft. A **Junior ADU (conversion within the existing primary structure)** capped at **500 sq ft** may be available in addition to a standard ADU depending on zoning district.
- **Setbacks:** typical range cited is **7–10 ft rear**, **3–5 ft side** — varies by zoning district; front setbacks are typically more restrictive and generally match the primary dwelling's zone standard. Confirm exact figures for the applicable zone.
- **Height:** detached ADUs must comply with the district's accessory-structure height limit — figure varies by zone, not independently confirmed in this pass.
- **Design compatibility:** materials, colors, and architectural style must match or complement the primary residence (citywide standard).
- **Parking:** **1 additional off-street space** generally required, consistent with the AB 396 cap; may vary slightly by zoning district.
- **Owner-occupancy:** Henderson's code has **historically required** owner-occupancy of either the primary home or the ADU. Given the 2025 AB 396-compliance amendment, **confirm current status directly with Henderson Development Services before relying on this** — this is exactly the kind of provision AB 396's minimum-compliance requirements could have touched.
- **Short-term-rental restriction:** governed by a **separate Henderson STR ordinance** — separate STR license required (occupancy limits, noise rules apply). Long-term leasing is permitted once a Certificate of Occupancy is issued.
- **Utility/sewer:** separate metering not required but recommended for third-party (tenant) rental scenarios; each utility connection requires its own permit; early coordination with utility providers advised.
- **# per lot:** generally **1 ADU per single-family lot**; a Junior ADU may be permitted in addition, depending on zoning district (i.e., potentially 2 accessory units of different types on one lot — confirm per zone).
- **Building code baseline:** 2021 International Building Code + applicable fire code, mandatory for permits issued after March 2023.
- **Permit path:** submit to the Development Services Center; review covers zoning compliance, building-code conformance, and design standards; over-the-counter review may be available for simpler designs.
- **Timeline:** pre-application consultation (1–2 weeks) → plan preparation (4–8 weeks) → permit application → plan review (4–8 weeks) → construction/inspections (4–8 months) → Certificate of Occupancy. **Total: roughly 12–18 months** (longer than the Clark County estimate above; likely reflects a more thorough Henderson design-review process — confirm current timeline, as this may compress post-2025 amendment).
- **Fees:** not specified in sources reviewed — request current fee schedule from Development Services.
- **Ministerial vs. discretionary:** not explicitly stated in sources reviewed; the standard building-permit path with possible over-the-counter review suggests a largely ministerial process for conforming designs, but this is inferred, not confirmed — verify directly.

Sources: [Steadily, "ADU Housing Laws and Regulations in Henderson - 2026"](https://www.steadily.com/blog/adu-laws-regulations-henderson); [Fox5 Vegas (2026-06-19)](https://www.fox5vegas.com/2026/06/19/nevada-casita-law-adds-more-housing-options-across-las-vegas/); [Henderson Development Code portal](https://www.cityofhenderson.com/government/development-code).

## 5. City of North Las Vegas (Municipal Code Title 17, Zoning)

- **Status relative to AB 396:** North Las Vegas city staff **confirmed its existing code already complied** with AB 396's minimum requirements as of the July 1, 2026 deadline — **no ordinance amendment was required.** This is the one jurisdiction of the four where the "casita law" did not trigger a code rewrite; existing Title 17 provisions were determined sufficient.
- **Governing chapters:** Title 17 (Zoning), specifically **Chapter 17.24 (Development Standards)** — relevant subsection referenced in research as **17.24.040 (Placement/Location standards)** — and **Chapter 17.20 (Use Regulations)**. Full code: [North Las Vegas Municode](https://library.municode.com/nv/north_las_vegas).
- **Permitted zones / dimensional standards:** ADUs/guest houses are permitted in residential zones as secondary dwelling units, but **exact size, setback, height, and parking figures vary by zoning district and were not extractable from available secondary sources in this pass** — Municode access was blocked (403) during this research session. **This is the weakest-sourced jurisdiction in this report; the owner should pull Chapter 17.24 and 17.20 directly (or via the Planning & Zoning counter) before relying on any dimensional assumption.**
- **General accessory-structure rule (Title 17, confirmed via search):** accessory buildings on the same lot must be separated from each other by **not less than 10 ft**; no cellar, garage, tent, unfinished-basement, accessory building, mobile home, or RV outside an approved park may be used as a dwelling unit **except** where the zoning code's ADU/guest-house provisions specifically authorize it.
- **Building code baseline:** 2018 International Residential Code referenced in secondary sources (confirm current adopted edition).
- **# per lot:** generally **1 ADU per single-family property** (consistent with the valley-wide pattern), per secondary-source summary — confirm in Title 17 directly.
- **Utility:** possible requirement for separate utility connections — not confirmed from primary code text in this pass.
- **Setbacks/height/coverage/parking/owner-occupancy/STR:** **NOT independently confirmed from primary code text in this research pass.** Do not assume parity with Las Vegas or Henderson figures. Flag as an open item for the owner or a follow-up research pass with direct Municode/city-counter access.
- **Permit path / fees / timeline / ministerial vs. discretionary:** **not confirmed in this pass** — contact North Las Vegas Planning & Zoning directly.

## 6. Cross-cutting notes

- **AB 396's kitchen/parking/HOA floor applies to all four jurisdictions** regardless of local code text: any local ordinance that (a) bans a full kitchen in a qualifying ADU, (b) requires more than 1 parking space, or (c) lets an HOA prohibit ADUs outright is superseded by state law as of July 1, 2026.
- **HOAs retain architectural-review authority** (materials, rooflines, landscape integration) in all four jurisdictions even where zoning is now permissive — this is a separate approval track from the municipal permit and was NOT touched by AB 396. Named examples of HOAs known to actively review casita/ADU projects: Summerlin, MacDonald Highlands, Anthem Country Club, Queensridge, Red Rock Country Club, Seven Hills.
- **"Casita" is not a uniform term.** In Clark County code it specifically means "no kitchen." In common market usage (and in the City of Las Vegas post-AB 396 framework) it's often used loosely to mean any backyard accessory unit, kitchen or not. Confirm which the owner intends and use the jurisdiction's own defined term when calling a planning counter.
- **Selling separately prohibited everywhere:** an ADU/casita/accessory apartment cannot be sold or subdivided off from the primary residence as a separate parcel, in every jurisdiction reviewed.
