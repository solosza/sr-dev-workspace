# City & County of Honolulu ADU Research

**Researched:** 2026-07-22
**Scope:** Jurisdiction-level rules only — no parcel-specific legal advice. Owner must confirm zoning district, lot size, existing dwelling count, and infrastructure capacity against the specific parcel with the Department of Planning and Permitting (DPP) before design/permit.

---

## 0. THE HEADLINE: two state mandates are forcing Honolulu's ADU code, on two different clocks

Unlike the Las Vegas valley's single forcing statute (AB 396), Honolulu's ADU rules are being reshaped by **two separate Hawaii state laws** on two different timelines:

- **Act 232 (2023), codified at HRS §46-4.8:** preempts county discretionary review for qualifying ADUs statewide — **requires ministerial approval** (no public hearing, no discretionary permit) for ADUs that meet the county's own zoning/dimensional standards. This is the state-law backstop that makes Honolulu's ADU permit path a standard building-permit review rather than a conditional-use process. [Source: search-derived from HRS §46-4.8 citation]
- **Act 39 / SB 3202 (2024):** requires **every Hawaii county to allow at least 2 ADUs per residential lot by December 31, 2026**. Enforcement mechanism: if a county has not adopted a compliant ordinance by that date, the county **cannot deny a permit application for up to 2 ADUs on the basis of exceeding maximum unit count** (a county may still deny on infrastructure, design, or development-standard grounds). [Sources: [HawaiiYIMBY, "Act 39 (SB 3202)"](https://hawaiiyimby.org/2024-state-legislation/act-39-sb-3202/); [SB3202 CD1 bill text](https://www.capitol.hawaii.gov/sessions/session2024/bills/SB3202_CD1_.HTM)]
- **Honolulu's response so far — Ordinance 25-2 (Bill 62), adopted January 3, 2025, most provisions effective September 30, 2025** — is explicitly **Phase 1** of compliance: it liberalized the existing 1-ADU-per-lot framework (simplified sizing, relaxed the old 3,500 sq ft lot-size floor, allowed ADUs on lots that already have an ʻohana unit or two principal dwellings). **It does NOT yet implement the Act 39 "2 ADUs per lot" mandate.** A Phase 2 ordinance to reach the 2-ADU standard is expected before the December 31, 2026 state deadline but **had not been located as adopted text as of this research date (2026-07-22)** — flag as PENDING and re-check DPP/City Council records close to the deadline.

**Status as of this research date:** Ordinance 25-2 (1-ADU framework) is currently in force. The 2-ADU mandate (Act 39) is **PROPOSED/PENDING at the county level with a hard state deadline of 2026-12-31** — treat any "2 ADU" claim for Honolulu as not-yet-locally-codified until a Phase 2 ordinance is confirmed.

Sources: [aduzoning.org, "Honolulu ADU Rules (2026)"](https://www.aduzoning.org/adu-rules/honolulu-hi/); [ArchitectHonolulu, "What's New With ADUs in Honolulu (2025 Ordinance 25-2 Update)"](https://www.architecthonolulu.com/post/whats-new-with-adus-in-honolulu-ordinance-25-2); [Ordinance 25-2 / ORD25-002 primary text (amlegal.com PDF)](https://files.amlegal.com/pdffiles/Honolulu/ORD25-002.pdf); [HawaiiYIMBY Act 39 summary](https://hawaiiyimby.org/2024-state-legislation/act-39-sb-3202/).

The Bill 20 figures are retained below only as the historical baseline, clearly marked, because older secondary sources online still describe the pre-2025 rules and could mislead a search.

## 2. Eligible zones + lot-size minimums

- **Zoning districts:** R-3.5, R-5, R-7.5, R-10, R-20 (Residential districts), plus the Country District (AG-1/AG-2 agricultural-residential). [Source: aduzoning.org]
- **Lot size minimum:** Bill 20 (2015, superseded) required a 3,500 sq ft minimum lot. **Ordinance 25-2 (2025, current) relaxed this floor** — secondary sources describe "even sub-3,500 sq ft lots may now qualify" for the smaller (500 sq ft) size tier, though the exact lower bound was **not independently confirmed against primary ROH text in this pass** — verify with DPP for any lot under 3,500 sq ft.
- **Eligibility gate unique to Honolulu (current, Ordinance 25-2):** an ADU may be added on a property that **already contains an ʻohana unit or two principal dwellings** — the 2025 rewrite explicitly opened this previously-blocked scenario. [Source: architecthonolulu.com]
- **CPR exclusion:** ADUs are **not allowed on Condominium Property Regime (CPR)–designated lots**, and conversely a CPR cannot be created on a lot that already has an ADU. [Source: architecthonolulu.com]

---

## 3. Max ADU size, attached/detached

- **Current (Ordinance 25-2, effective 2025-09-30):**
  - Lots **under 5,000 sq ft** → ADU capped at **500 sq ft**
  - Lots **5,000 sq ft or larger** → ADU capped at **1,000 sq ft**
  - Same cap applies whether attached or detached. [Sources: aduzoning.org; architecthonolulu.com]
- **Historical (Bill 20, 2015, superseded — for reference only):** lots 3,500–4,999 sq ft → 400 sq ft cap; lots ≥5,000 sq ft → 800 sq ft cap. Do not use these figures for any permit filed after 2025-09-30.
- **Attached / detached / conversion:** both attached and detached ADUs are permitted; conversion of existing structures (e.g., garage) is allowed as long as the resulting unit conforms to current dimensional and life-safety standards. Hawaii's local equivalent of a "junior ADU" is the separately defined **"ʻohana unit"** category (a distinct, older dwelling-unit type under ROH 21-10.1, not identical to an ADU) — confirm with DPP which category applies if a smaller in-structure conversion is intended.
- **Required unit components (both Bill 20 baseline and current):** full kitchen (with oven), full bathroom, sleeping area, and at least one dedicated parking stall — this is what makes it a "complete" ADU unit under the code definition.

## 4. Setbacks / height / lot coverage

- **Setbacks:** general zoning/building-code setbacks apply; one secondary source cites a **5 ft minimum from rear and side property lines** for the ADU structure — **this figure was not independently verified against primary ROH text in this pass**, treat as indicative only and confirm with DPP for the applicable zoning district before design.
- **Height:** one secondary source cites **up to 25 ft for a detached ADU**, with an attached ADU following the primary residence's applicable zoning height limit — **not independently confirmed from primary code text**, verify with DPP.
- **Lot coverage / FAR:** Ordinance 25-2's own summary text states plainly that **"all zoning and building standards continue to apply: setbacks, height, lot coverage/FAR (where applicable), access, and life-safety"** — i.e., the ordinance did not create ADU-specific overrides to these dimensional standards; the ADU must fit within the zoning district's existing coverage/FAR envelope alongside the primary dwelling(s). Exact figures are district-specific — pull from ROH Chapter 21's use-table for the applicable zone.
- **Bathrooms:** a general per-dwelling bathroom cap (commonly cited as up to 3) applies but was not confirmed as ADU-specific in this pass.

## 5. Parking

- **Standard requirement:** **one off-street parking stall per ADU** (compact-size stalls are allowed). [Sources: aduzoning.org; architecthonolulu.com]
- **Waivers:** the stall requirement is **waived within 800 ft of a bus stop** (Primary Urban Center / Ewa areas) or **within 0.5 miles of a rail (Skyline) station**, **provided the property has not already used a prior ʻohana-unit parking adjustment**. [Sources: aduzoning.org; atlasconstruction808.com]
- Note: this is functionally the same "1-stall cap" concept as Nevada's AB 396 statewide maximum, but Honolulu's rule is framed as a **requirement with transit-proximity waivers**, not a state-imposed ceiling on what a city may demand.

---

## 6. Owner-occupancy requirement

- **Current status: owner-occupancy IS required.** Either the primary residence or the ADU must be occupied by the property owner or an immediate family member. [Sources: aduzoning.org; atlasconstruction808.com]
- **Mechanism:** historically (Bill 20 era / Ordinance 15-41) this was enforced via a **recorded covenant** (filed with the Bureau of Conveyances) restricting occupancy/use. One secondary source flagged that **Ordinance 25-2's covenant terms may have been modified or omitted relative to the prior form** — **this is unconfirmed; verify the current DPP covenant/deed-restriction requirement directly before relying on either the "covenant required" or "covenant removed" framing.**

---

## 7. Short-term-rental (STR) restriction

- **ADU-specific:** minimum rental term of **6 months**; short-term/vacation rental use of an ADU is **prohibited**. [Sources: aduzoning.org; atlasconstruction808.com]
- **Citywide context (not ADU-specific, but relevant if the owner is weighing rental strategy):** under a separate, broader STR ordinance (**CO-25-02 / Bill 62's STR provisions**, effective ~September 2025), all non-bed-and-breakfast, non-conforming-use-certificate residential rentals citywide are subject to a **90-consecutive-day minimum rental term**. Bed-and-breakfast use (a distinct licensed category, capped at 2 rooms / 2 adult occupants each, owner-present, primary-residence tax-exemption required) is a narrow exception and is **not the same category as an ADU rental**. [Source: [HawaiiLiving, "Honolulu's New 90-Day Minimum Rental Rule"](https://www.hawaiiliving.com/blog/90-day-min-rental-terms/)]
- **Net effect:** an ADU cannot be used as a nightly/weekly short-term rental under either the ADU-specific 6-month rule or the citywide 90-day floor — whichever applies, both are well above any STR-typical term.

## 8. Water/sewer capacity rules — Honolulu's known infrastructure constraint

This is the dimension flagged in the task spec as a known Honolulu-specific issue, and research confirms it is a **hard procedural gate**, not a soft consideration:

- **Pre-Form / early sign-off requirement:** before submitting a building-permit application, the owner must submit an **ʻOhana/ADU Pre-Form** to DPP and obtain **written sign-off from multiple agencies**: the **Board of Water Supply** (water pressure/capacity), the **Department of Environmental Services** and/or **State Department of Health** (sewer/wastewater capacity, or approved onsite system), the **Department of Transportation Services** (traffic impact), and **Honolulu Fire Department**. [Sources: architecthonolulu.com; general-search synthesis citing DPP Pre-Form process]
- **The Pre-Form does NOT replace the permit process** — it is described explicitly as "an early check," with full standard permit review still required afterward.
- **Cesspool / onsite-system constraint:** Hawaii is statewide phasing out cesspools. If a public sewer main is available, adding a second dwelling unit can **trigger a mandatory sewer connection**. Where no public sewer is available, State Department of Health rules govern onsite disposal/septic upgrades. **A single wastewater system (septic or cesspool) can serve a maximum of 5 bedrooms total across all dwelling units on the property** — this is a hard cap that can independently block an ADU regardless of zoning eligibility if the primary home is already bedroom-heavy. [Source: search-derived, general Hawaii wastewater-capacity guidance — verify current DOH figure for the specific parcel]
- **Practical implication:** for a Honolulu property, the wastewater/septic bedroom-count cap and the Board of Water Supply capacity sign-off should be checked **before** any design work, since either can be the binding constraint ahead of zoning dimensional limits.

## 9. Number of ADUs per lot

- **Current (Ordinance 25-2 / Phase 1):** **maximum 1 ADU per property.**
- **Pending (Act 39 state mandate, deadline 2026-12-31):** Honolulu is required to allow **at least 2 ADUs per residential lot** by that date via a Phase 2 ordinance. **Not yet locally codified as of this research date** — see §0 above.

## 10. Permit path, fees, timeline, ministerial vs. discretionary

- **Ministerial vs. discretionary:** **ministerial**, as a matter of *state law* — Hawaii Act 232 (2023), codified at **HRS §46-4.8**, preempts county discretionary review and requires ministerial (no public hearing) approval for ADUs that meet the county's own zoning/dimensional standards. This applies on top of, and independent from, Honolulu's own ordinance text. [Source: search-derived citation of HRS §46-4.8; **primary state-statute text not independently pulled in this pass — recommend confirming exact HRS §46-4.8 language before citing to a permit counter**]
- **Permit path:**
  1. Owner submits the **ʻOhana/ADU Pre-Form** to DPP, triggering the multi-agency infrastructure sign-off described in §8.
  2. Standard DPP building-permit application and plan review (zoning compliance, building-code conformance).
  3. Recording of the owner-occupancy covenant (mechanism currently unconfirmed post-Ordinance-25-2, see §6).
  4. Construction with standard inspections (framing, electrical, plumbing, final).
  5. Certificate of completion/occupancy.
- **Fees:** **valuation-based**, no fixed schedule found in this pass — one secondary source estimates **$3,000–$8,000** all-in, but explicitly recommends contacting DPP directly for the current fee schedule since it updates periodically. Treat as a rough planning figure only.
- **Timeline:** wide range reported — **90–120 days typical** for straightforward applications with clear infrastructure capacity (per aduzoning.org); another source describes simple cases moving through "in a few months" but complex projects or high DPP volume periods pushing **past a year**. Pre-approved/standard plan sets reportedly cut review time significantly. **No single authoritative timeline confirmed — expect 3–12+ months depending on infrastructure sign-off complexity and DPP backlog.**
- **Utility connection cost (separate from permit fee):** electrical panel upgrades commonly cited at **$5,000–$15,000**; sewer/water connection costs not independently confirmed for Honolulu in this pass (compare to Clark County's $8,000–$20,000 sewer-lateral figure as a rough cross-jurisdiction sanity check only — Honolulu figures may differ materially).

## 11. Cross-cutting notes

- **Two-track state pressure, two different problems:** Act 232/HRS §46-4.8 addresses *process* (forces ministerial approval, no discretionary hearing) — already in effect. Act 39 addresses *quantity* (forces 2-ADU-per-lot allowance) — deadline 2026-12-31, not yet locally implemented. Don't conflate the two when discussing "what Hawaii law requires."
- **Bill 20 (2015) figures are obsolete for new applications** as of 2025-09-30 (Ordinance 25-2 effective date) — any source citing the 3,500 sq ft lot minimum or 400/800 sq ft size caps as current is describing the superseded framework.
- **"Ohana unit" ≠ "ADU"** in Honolulu's own code vocabulary — ROH 21-10.1 treats them as related but distinct defined dwelling-unit categories with separate historical rule sets (ʻohana provisions predate the ADU ordinance). Confirm which category applies to any existing secondary structure on the parcel before assuming ADU rules govern it.
- **Gaps requiring direct owner follow-up (flagged throughout, consolidated here):**
  1. Exact current lot-size floor under Ordinance 25-2 (if any) for lots under 3,500 sq ft — not independently confirmed from primary ROH text.
  2. Exact setback (rear/side/front) and height figures per zoning district — one secondary-source figure (5 ft setback, 25 ft height) unverified against primary code.
  3. Current status of the owner-occupancy recorded covenant under Ordinance 25-2 (retained vs. modified vs. removed) — conflicting signal in secondary sources.
  4. Exact DPP fee schedule (current, valuation-based) — request directly from DPP.
  5. Status/adoption date of the Phase 2 "2 ADUs per lot" ordinance required by Act 39 (deadline 2026-12-31) — check City Council legislative records (hnldoc.ehawaii.gov) close to the deadline; as of this research date no Phase 2 ordinance was located.
