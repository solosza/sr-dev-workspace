# Competition — Paid "adversarially-vetted business ideas" feed

**Date:** 2026-07-30
**Verdict:** GO-IF (leaning kill) — hard, funded arena with a look-alike incumbent. Only enter via a narrow contrarian seam + your own distribution; head-on = don't.

## The one real gap
Every player sells ideas as **optimism** ("here's your next winner"). **Nobody leads with adversarial killing** ("here's why 47 of 50 are traps"). The skeptic / anti-hype position + the *killed-ideas* content is genuine white space — but it's a **positioning** seam, not a moat (IdeaBrowser could bolt on a "risks" section tomorrow).

## Your angle to win (if pursued)
- **Brand it "the idea graveyard"** — the only feed that *kills*. Contrarian to the whole category.
- **Pick a niche** (e.g. only AI-agent businesses, or only local-service) — don't fight their 1,000-idea volume.
- **Killed-ideas = free top-of-funnel** (the debunk content nobody else makes) → feeds the paid list.
- **Don't compete on:** idea volume or brand — IdeaBrowser wins both.

## The killer risk
**No distribution/brand.** IdeaBrowser rides Greg Isenberg's audience; your differentiator (adversarial vetting) is copyable. Distribution decides this — and you don't have it yet. That's what the deep-dive must test.

## Rivals

| Rival | Their edge | Their hole |

## Arena structure
Five Forces: rivalry HIGH (funded incumbent) · new entrants HIGH (anyone can newsletter) · substitutes HIGH (free podcasts, AI) · buyer power HIGH ($10/mo, cheap switching) · supplier power LOW (you own the engine). **Hard arena.**

## Watch
IdeaBrowser feature moves (esp. any "risks/why-not" addition) + new "anti-hype" entrants — monthly.

**HITL:** commit the graveyard/niche angle · park · kill (arena too hard without distribution).
