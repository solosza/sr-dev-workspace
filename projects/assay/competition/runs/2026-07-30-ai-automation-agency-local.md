# Competition — Governed automation for a regulated local niche

**Date:** 2026-07-30 · **Business:** the assay reframe (governed/productized automation, one regulated niche)
**Verdict:** GO-IF — winnable in a narrow regulated niche where governance is the moat; the generic arena is a bloodbath, don't enter there.

## The gap
The whole AAA crowd chases *easy* automations (chatbots, lead-gen) for *easy* niches. **Almost nobody does governed, audit-trailed automation for regulated local businesses** (dental/medical/legal/financial) — because compliance is hard and "boring." That's the seam, and it's exactly where your kernel (audit + mandatory HITL) is a real, hard-to-copy edge — not a claim.

## Your angle to win
Governed automation + an **audit trail + human-approval** for one regulated niche, sold as an outcome (hours saved / revenue recovered) with a guarantee. Don't compete on price or generic workflows.

## Killer risk
Same as always: **reachability** of local SMBs, and the underlying tools (n8n/Claude) are commodity. Moat = governance + niche + outcome-guarantee, not the automation itself.

## Rivals
| Rival | Edge | Hole |
|-------|------|------|
| Thousands of AAA freelancers/micro-agencies | cheap, fast | generic, no governance, no niche depth, churn |
| Zapier / Make / n8n (DIY) | cheap, self-serve | the SMB won't/can't build + govern it |
| Vertical SaaS (e.g. dental tools) | niche-native | not custom automation; gaps between tools |
| Established automation agencies | enterprise-grade | ignore small regulated local shops |

Five Forces: rivalry HIGH · substitutes HIGH (DIY) · buyer power MED (regulated buyers stickier) · new entrants HIGH · supplier power LOW. **Hard generic arena; a defensible regulated niche.**

## Watch
Vertical SaaS adding AI + governance; any AAA operator niching into compliance. Monthly.
