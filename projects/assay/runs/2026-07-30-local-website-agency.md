# Assay Run — "Build-first" local website agency

**Date:** 2026-07-30
**Idea (plain):** Find local businesses on Google Maps that have reviews but no website, auto-build them a site, cold-outreach the live link, sell setup + recurring.
**Verdict:** Original plan too crowded / structurally broken — but three better versions found inside it.
**Best move:** Test "sell the leads" (Idea 2) this week; if there's appetite, build "get-found + never-miss-a-call" (Idea 1) as the real recurring business.

> This file consolidates two in-session runs of the same idea: **v1** (first pass) and **v2** (re-run after the engine was upgraded to force the transpose + payer-swap angles and add TAM-density + reachability gates). The v2 result is the headline below; the evolution note at the end records what changed.

---

## The answer, in plain language

**Your first version won't win — but the tool found three better versions hiding inside it, and one you can test this week without building a single website.**

The thing that sinks the original plan: **the people you're targeting have no website *because* they barely use the internet.** So emailing them a link mostly won't reach them — you'd be chasing the exact people who are hardest to reach online. The better ideas below each get around that.

### The original plan — DON'T do it
*Find no-website businesses, build a site, cold-email the link, sell it.*
**Why it dies:** everyone already does this; the "website" is now a $15 app anyone can use; the customers are cheap and churn fast; and you can't reliably reach people who aren't online. Skip it.

### Idea 1 — "Get found + never miss a call" (the real business)
**What:** Pick one trade (e.g. mobile mechanics). Don't sell a website — sell **getting found on Google + an AI that answers the phone and books the job when they're too busy.**
**Why it works:** a missed call = a lost $300 job, and they feel it. Monthly fee ($200–500), it sticks (it's booking their jobs), and you reach them *by phone* — the call *is* the demo.
**What kills it:** if the trade you pick doesn't actually miss calls, or won't pay monthly.
**Cheapest test:** one trade, one city, call 40 this week. Goal: **1 paying customer at $200+/mo within 14 days.** ~$0.

### Idea 2 — Sell the *leads*, not the websites (the sneaky-smart one)
**What:** Your machine finds profitable local businesses with weak online presence. **That list is worth money to others** — website companies (Wix, GoDaddy), Google partner programs, franchise HQs, other agencies. Sell them the leads. You build/host nothing.
**Why it works:** no sites to make, and you now sell to **big companies that are easy to reach** instead of chasing shop owners who aren't. Works nationwide instead of running out of businesses in one city.
**What kills it:** the leads must be genuinely better than what buyers already get.
**Cheapest test:** build 100 great leads in one niche, pitch **one** buyer. Goal: **1 buyer pays within 14 days.** ~$0, touches zero hard-to-reach owners — why it's the smartest *first* test.

### Idea 3 — Fix their Google, not their website
**What:** Most "invisible" local businesses lose customers on **Google Maps** (unclaimed/weak listing, no photos, wrong hours), not on a website. Fix + manage it monthly.
**Why it works:** far more businesses have a weak Google listing than none-at-all — bigger pool — and Maps is where people actually find local shops now.
**Best use:** this is the "get found" half of Idea 1 — run them together.

*(Parked: partner with a company that already sells to these shops — a booking or POS vendor — for warm intros. Solves the reach problem entirely, but you'd have to land a partner first. Revisit once something above works.)*

### Bottom line
- Biggest business = **Idea 1**. Cheapest, safest first test = **Idea 2**. Smart sequence: test Idea 2 → if appetite, build Idea 1.

## Under the hood (the structured run)

**Normalized idea:** value = *get invisible local businesses found + never miss a lead* · who-pays = *SMB (v1) → re-examined to other buyers (v2)* · mechanism = *find under-represented business → auto-build the missing asset → pitch it built → sell.*

**Wedges (v2):**

| Wedge | Angle | Verdict |
|-------|-------|---------|
| Original: no-website + one-time sites + cold outreach | literal | **KILLED** — saturation, no moat, one-shot, can't reach digitally-disengaged targets, exhausts a metro fast |
| Idea 1: AI receptionist / never-miss-a-call, presence = free hook | recombine | **BUILD** — real budget, sticky, reachable by phone |
| Idea 2: sell qualified leads to Wix/Google/franchise/agencies | **payer-swap** | **BUILD** — lightest, national reach, dodges the reachability + TAM gates |
| Idea 3: fix the Google Business Profile, not the website | **transpose** | **BUILD** — bigger pool than websites; pairs with Idea 1 |
| Warm channel partner (POS/booking vendor) | constraint-break | pass — needs a partner not yet held; scale unlock |
| Sell the find+demo pipeline as SaaS to agencies | invert | pass — legal facilitation risk + high build cost |

**Cheapest decision-changing tests:** Idea 2 → 100 leads, pitch 1 buyer, ≥1 pays in 14 days (~$0, no SMB contact). Idea 1 → call 40 in one niche, ≥1 paying $200+/mo in 14 days, ≥7% reply-to-call.

**Decision:** conditional-green on Idea 1 (rank 1, biggest business) and Idea 2 (rank 2, cheapest first experiment); Idea 3 folds into Idea 1; partner + SaaS parked. No unconditional green — demand is untested but cheaply testable.

## How this evolved (v1 → v2)

**v1** correctly killed the naive plan and found the "flip to recurring + one niche" reframe — but it was creatively shallow: every option was the *same* website-for-SMB business re-dressed, and it missed a near-fatal flaw.

**v2** (after upgrading the engine) added:
- **Forced "transpose"** → surfaced **Idea 3** (fix the Google listing, not the website — a bigger, better pool).
- **Forced "payer-swap"** → surfaced **Idea 2** (sell leads to a different, reachable, budgeted buyer) — now arguably the best *first* move, and v1 missed it entirely.
- **New "reachability" gate** → demoted the original plan from "crowded" to "structurally broken" (you can't reach people who aren't online).
- **New "TAM-density" gate** → flagged that one-metro plays exhaust fast, which is why the national lead-resale angle scores higher.

The upgrade paid off: the two strongest ideas both came from the newly-forced lenses.
