The nurse sheet is customer #1.

## /data — monetize the data this generates
**Play:** every build logs a real "tiny manual process + the tool that fixed it." Over time this becomes a proprietary taxonomy of the busywork small operators actually have (and what they will pay to remove).
**Upside:** sell it three ways — (1) a benchmark ("here is how 500 small care teams schedule"), (2) a demand-signal feed (what people ask for next is a build roadmap you can sell to vertical-SaaS players), (3) an uncopyable internal catalog that makes your own next build faster and cheaper.
**Non-obvious:** the data is the exhaust that becomes the map. The tools are almost incidental; the record of what-the-long-tail-needs is the durable asset.

## /distribution — the lanes that make it big
**Play:** referral is the seed. The scalable lanes: (a) a small "made by [you]" footer on every deployed tool, so each live tool recruits the next non-technical owner who sees it in the wild; (b) partner with the trusted aggregators of each niche (associations, franchisors, local Facebook/church networks); (c) build-in-public video (the nurse story) so the demos themselves are the marketing.
**Upside:** zero ad spend, compounding. Each tool is simultaneously the product and the billboard.
**Non-obvious:** you are a "be seen solving" channel, not an ads channel. Distribution is a by-product of delivery, not a separate budget line.

## /platform — become the rails others build on
**Play:** an intake layer ("describe your annoyance") that routes a plain-language problem to an auto-generated tool plus hosting. Open it so others (virtual assistants, bookkeepers, local consultants) build ON your rails for their own clients.
**Upside:** you stop being the builder and become the pipe plus the hosting margin. AI does the labor; you own the toll booth.
**Non-obvious:** when the cost of building software approaches zero, the winner is whoever owns demand-routing (knows who needs what), not whoever builds. The platform is a bet on that shift.

## /productize — service to product (escape hours-for-dollars)
**Play:** take the ONE proven niche tool (care-team scheduling) and turn it into a repeatable, self-serve product at a monthly price. Then repeat per niche with that niche's most-requested tool.
**Upside:** build once, sell many. Escapes hours-for-dollars and creates the clean, transferable asset a buyer would actually pay a multiple for.
**Non-obvious:** this is the escape hatch from the services trap AND the single highest-leverage move — it also seeds the data, platform, and license angles at once.

## /license — franchise / white-label the method
**Play:** franchise the whole playbook (templates + tech + brand + hosting) to people who own local trust you do not — a nurse-influencer for care teams, a trades-YouTuber for contractors. They supply the relationships and delivery; you supply the machine and take a royalty on their builds and hosting.
**Upside:** converts your solo bottleneck into other people's throughput, without you becoming a course-seller.
**Non-obvious:** this is the inverted, non-guru version of "agency-in-a-box." You license access to trust and a working machine, not a how-to PDF. (The assay killed the guru version; this is what survives when you flip it.)

## /stack — adjacent revenue on the SAME customer
**Play:** land with one tool, then sell the same customer their whole small-ops spine: the tool + hosting + tweaks + a domain + email + a simple website + an "on-call tech person" retainer.
**Upside:** lifetime value multiplies; you become their outsourced tech department and own the operating spine.
**Non-obvious:** the first tool is a beachhead into being their system-of-record, not a one-off sale. The retainer is where a project business becomes a recurring one.

## /adjacent — adjacent VENTURES in the space (feed /assay)
**Play:** the ventures the map reveals, each a candidate to route back into /assay: (a) a marketplace of the top-N niche micro-tools; (b) "tools-as-a-perk" sold to banks, insurers, or chambers of commerce for their SMB customers (a payer-swap venture); (c) own one niche's system-of-record end to end.
**Upside:** each is a standalone business, not a feature.
**Non-obvious:** the strongest adjacent is the payer-swap (sell to the aggregator of the underserved), because it fixes reachability AND the solo bottleneck at the same time.
