# AI Business Models — Revenue Evidence vs Hype

## Summary

Evaluating AI business models with documented revenue evidence. Separating real from hype. Cross-referencing with our existing capabilities to identify the highest-leverage paths.

## Models with Real Revenue Evidence

### 1. AI Agent SaaS (Outcome-Based Pricing)

**Evidence:**
- Salesforce Agentforce: $800M ARR by end of fiscal 2026 (169% YoY growth, 29K deals in Q4)
- Intercom Fin AI agent: 9-figure revenue charging $0.99 per resolved support ticket
- Anthropic Claude Code: ~$1B annualized revenue within 6 months of launch

**Model:** Charge per resolution, per action, or per outcome instead of per seat. Hybrid models combining subscription base + usage fees are winning.

**Margin profile:** High (70-85% gross margin for SaaS, even higher for per-outcome)

**Time to first dollar:** 3-6 months (need working product + first customer)

**Applies to us:** Yes — QA platforms could charge per test suite generated, SSH compliance per audit run, kernel per agent produced.

### 2. AI-Augmented Consulting (Services Delivered Faster)

**Evidence:**
- Deloitte, Accenture, and BCG all report AI integration into consulting delivery
- BCG published "The AI-First SaaS Company" playbook (2026)
- Solo consultants report 3-5x throughput using AI agents for delivery

**Model:** Charge consulting rates but deliver using AI agents. Human expertise for scoping and quality, AI for execution. Margin expands because delivery cost drops.

**Margin profile:** Very high (traditional consulting: 30-50% margin; AI-augmented: 60-80%)

**Time to first dollar:** Immediate if you have a network; 1-3 months cold

**Applies to us:** Yes — RT automation consulting, QA platform implementation, compliance auditing. The kernel IS the delivery mechanism.

### 3. AI Infrastructure / Developer Tools

**Evidence:**
- Genspark (super-agent platform): $200M+ annualized revenue, raised $385M Series B (April 2026)
- Cursor, Windsurf, and other AI coding tools generating significant revenue
- Open-source + commercial license model gaining traction

**Model:** Open-source the core, charge for enterprise features, hosted service, or support.

**Margin profile:** Very high for SaaS (80%+), lower for support/consulting

**Time to first dollar:** 6-12 months (need community adoption first)

**Applies to us:** Partial — the kernel could follow this model, but community building is slow. Better as a long-term play.

### 4. Government Contracting with AI Delivery

**Evidence:**
- Federal AI spending increasing year-over-year
- Small business set-asides specifically for IT/AI services
- @WritesToProfit model (backlog 092 source) — subcontract model has constraints (FAR 52.219-14, 50% rule)

**Model:** Win govcon contracts, deliver using AI agents. The 50% rule means you must perform at least 50% of the work with your own labor (which includes your AI-augmented labor).

**Margin profile:** Medium-high (govcon rates: $150-300/hr, AI-augmented delivery cost: much lower)

**Time to first dollar:** 3-6 months (LLC + SAM.gov + first contract)

**Applies to us:** Yes — SSH compliance, QA automation, and general IT consulting all have federal contract demand.

## Models with Weak/No Revenue Evidence

### 5. AI Content Creation / Course Selling

**Evidence:** Mostly Twitter claims. No verified revenue figures from independent sources. High volume of "I made $10K/month with AI" posts with no verification.

**Applies to us:** No. This is a distraction from our core capabilities.

### 6. AI Wrapper Apps (Thin Layer Over APIs)

**Evidence:** Race to the bottom. Most wrapper apps commoditized within months. Margins collapse as API providers add features.

**Applies to us:** No. We build infrastructure, not wrappers.

### 7. Prompt Engineering as a Service

**Evidence:** Initial hype has faded. Commoditized as models improve at following instructions. No sustainable moat.

**Applies to us:** No.

## The @WritesToProfit Thesis — Verdict

The original thesis ("set up an LLC, use AI to build businesses") is **directionally correct but oversimplified**:

- **Correct:** Form an LLC immediately. AI dramatically reduces the cost of delivery. Multiple revenue streams are viable under one entity.
- **Oversimplified:** "Just subcontract govcon work" ignores FAR 52.219-14 (50% rule). "Use AI to make money" without specifying a model is vague. Most of the specific tactics in the thread are hype (course selling, content farms, prompt engineering).
- **What's actually actionable:** Use AI to deliver consulting faster, build SaaS products that charge per outcome, and pursue govcon contracts where AI-augmented delivery gives you a cost advantage.

## Recommendation

**Priority order for revenue streams:**

1. **AI-augmented consulting** (immediate revenue, highest near-term ROI) — RT automation, QA implementation
2. **AI Agent SaaS** (medium-term, recurring revenue) — QA platforms, SSH compliance as per-outcome SaaS
3. **Government contracting** (medium-term, high-value contracts) — requires LLC + SAM.gov + first contract win
4. **Agent factory / kernel licensing** (long-term, platform play) — build community, then monetize
