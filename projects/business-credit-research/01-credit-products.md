# Credit Products — Business Cards vs Lines of Credit vs SBA Microloans

## Research Date
2026-05-28

## Executive Summary

For a new LLC (< 1 year old), **business credit cards are the most accessible product** — they require no minimum revenue, no operating history, and offer 0% intro APR for 12-18 months. Business lines of credit require $50K+ revenue and 6+ months in business. SBA microloans go up to $50K and are available to startups but require working with intermediary lenders.

### What a New LLC Can Expect

| Personal Credit Score | Expected Limit (per card) | Typical Approval |
|----------------------|---------------------------|------------------|
| 750+ (Excellent) | $10K-$30K | High likelihood |
| 700-749 (Good) | $5K-$15K | Good likelihood |
| 670-699 (Fair) | $3K-$10K | Moderate likelihood |
| Below 670 | $1K-$5K | Limited options |

### Key Features
- **No minimum revenue required** — accessible to brand-new businesses
- **No minimum time in business** — day-one LLC can apply
- **Personal guarantee required** — most business cards require the owner to personally guarantee
- **Reports to business credit bureaus** — builds business credit profile
- **0% intro APR** — interest-free financing for 12-18 months
- **Cash advances** — available but at much higher APR (typically 25%+), no intro period

### Limitations
- Credit limits are per-card, typically $5K-$30K
- Cash advances incur fees (3-5%) and higher APR from day one
- Personal guarantee means personal liability on default
- Minimum payments required monthly (typically 1-2% of balance)

## 2. Business Lines of Credit

### Qualification Requirements

| Lender Type | Min Credit Score | Min Time in Business | Min Revenue |
|-------------|-----------------|---------------------|-------------|
| Banks (Chase, Wells Fargo) | 680+ | 2+ years | $100K+/year |
| Online lenders (Lendio, BlueVine) | 580+ | 6+ months | $50K+/year |
| Alternative lenders | 550+ | 3+ months | $10K+/month deposits |

### How They Differ from Cards
- **Draw and repay** — like a credit card but for cash, not purchases
- **Higher limits** — $10K-$250K typical range
- **Interest on drawn amount only** — pay interest only on what you use
- **Monthly/weekly repayment** — more structured than credit card minimums
- **Better for large expenses** — rent, payroll, equipment

### Reality for New LLCs
**Business lines of credit are NOT accessible to new LLCs** in most cases. The 6-month minimum with $50K+ revenue requirement eliminates day-one startups. Bank LOCs require 2+ years and $100K+ revenue.

**Best path:** Start with credit cards → build 6-12 months of revenue → apply for LOC.

## 3. SBA Microloans

### Program Details

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Maximum amount | **$50,000** |
| Average loan | ~$13,000 |
| Interest rate | 8-13% |
| Maximum term | 6-7 years (up to 10 for some) |
| Application through | SBA-approved intermediary lenders |
| Available to startups? | **Yes** — designed for new businesses |

### Advantages
- Available to new businesses with no revenue history
- Lower interest rates than credit cards (8-13% vs 17-25%)
- Intermediary provides business guidance and mentorship
- Builds SBA lending relationship for future 7(a) loans

### Disadvantages
- Maximum $50K — insufficient for $250K target
- Longer approval process (weeks to months)
- Requires business plan and financial projections
- Limited intermediary availability by geography
- Can't use for paying yourself a salary directly (must be for business expenses)

## 5. Verdict: Which Products for a New LLC?

### Day 1 (LLC formation)
- **Business credit cards** — only realistic option
- Apply for 3-5 cards across different banks
- Target 0% intro APR cards exclusively
- Expected total credit: $15K-$100K (depending on personal credit)

### Month 6+ (with revenue)
- **Online business LOC** — once you have $50K+ annual revenue
- Higher limits, better rates than cards after intro period ends

### Anytime (with business plan)
- **SBA microloan** — if you need structured capital up to $50K
- Best for specific business investments, not salary
