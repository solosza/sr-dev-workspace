# Risks and Costs — Personal Guarantee, Default, True Cost

## Research Date
2026-05-28

## Executive Summary

The credit stacking strategy carries **significant personal financial risk**. Personal guarantees mean your personal assets are on the line. The true cost of carrying $150K at 20% APR is **$2,500/month in interest alone** — and the @hxxntrr "$7K/month salary for 3 years" math **does not work** at post-intro APR rates unless the business generates revenue to service the debt.

---

## 1. Personal Guarantee — You ARE Personally Liable

### The Reality
Almost every business credit card requires a **personal guarantee**. This means:

- The LLC's liability protection **does not protect you** from credit card debt
- If the business fails, creditors pursue **you personally**
- They can sue you, garnish wages, place liens on personal property
- Your home, savings, and personal assets are at risk

### What Happens on Default

| Stage | What Happens | Timeline |
|-------|-------------|----------|
| Missed payment | Late fee ($40-50), penalty APR applied | Day 1 |
| 30 days late | Reported to credit bureaus (personal) | Day 30 |
| 60 days late | Account flagged, collection calls begin | Day 60 |
| 90 days late | Account may be closed, charged off | Day 90 |
| 120-180 days | Sent to collections or lawsuit filed | 4-6 months |
| Lawsuit | Creditor sues you personally (personal guarantee) | 6-12 months |
| Judgment | Wage garnishment, bank account levy, property lien | 12+ months |

### Business Bankruptcy Does NOT Protect You
Because of the personal guarantee, **business bankruptcy does not eliminate your personal obligation**. You would need to file **personal bankruptcy** (Chapter 7 or 13) to discharge the debt.

### Personal Bankruptcy Consequences
- Stays on credit report for **7-10 years**
- Limits ability to get credit, start businesses, get certain jobs
- Chapter 7: Liquidates assets, discharges debt in ~4 months
- Chapter 13: Restructures debt into 3-5 year repayment plan

## 2. True Cost — The Math

### During 0% Intro Period (Months 1-12)

Assuming $150K stack (realistic first round with 750 FICO):

| Item | Monthly | Annual |
|------|---------|--------|
| Total credit available | $150,000 | — |
| Interest cost | **$0** | **$0** |
| Minimum payments (1-2% of balance) | **$1,500-$3,000** | $18,000-$36,000 |
| Net available for salary (after minimums) | $4,000-$5,500/mo | — |

**During intro period:** The model works. You pay minimums and use remaining credit for operating expenses and owner draws.

### After Intro Period Expires (Month 13+)

Assuming $150K balance remaining at average 20% APR:

| Item | Monthly | Annual |
|------|---------|--------|
| Balance | $150,000 | — |
| **Monthly interest** | **$2,500** | **$30,000** |
| Minimum payment (2% of balance + interest) | **$5,500** | $66,000 |
| Of which goes to principal | $3,000 | — |
| Of which is pure interest | $2,500 | — |

### The $250K Scenario (If You Hit Max Stack)

| Item | Monthly (at 20% APR) | Annual |
|------|---------------------|--------|
| Balance | $250,000 | — |
| **Monthly interest** | **$4,175** | **$50,100** |
| Minimum payment | **$9,175** | $110,100 |
| Of which goes to principal | $5,000 | — |
| Of which is pure interest | $4,175 | — |

### Total Cost Over 3 Years

| Scenario | Total Interest Paid | Total Payments |
|----------|-------------------|----------------|
| $150K, pay off in 3 years | ~$50,000 | ~$200,000 |
| $250K, pay off in 3 years | ~$83,000 | ~$333,000 |
| $150K, minimum payments only | ~$45,000/yr (barely touches principal) | Never pays off |

## 3. Can You Pay Yourself $7K/Month? — The @hxxntrr Claim

### The Claim
"Take $250K from banks, pay yourself $7K/month salary for 3 years"

### The Math

**$7K/month × 36 months = $252,000 total draw**

| Phase | Reality |
|-------|---------|
| Months 1-12 (0% APR) | **Works.** Draw $7K, pay minimums ($2,500-$5,000). Net burn: $9,500-$12,000/month of credit. |
| Month 12 | You've used ~$114K-$144K of your credit. Remaining: $106K-$136K. |
| Months 13-24 (20% APR) | **Breaks.** Interest on remaining $100K+ = $1,667-$2,267/mo. Plus minimums. Plus $7K draw. Monthly burn: $10K+. |
| Month 24 | Credit likely exhausted. Interest compounding. |
| Months 25-36 | **No credit left.** You're now servicing $150K+ in debt at 20% APR with no income from credit. |

### Verdict on the Claim
The $7K/month salary works for **12-18 months** (during 0% intro). After that, **interest destroys the math** unless your business is generating enough revenue to cover both the salary AND debt service.

**The claim is misleading.** It works only if:
1. You stack $250K+ (high end, not guaranteed)
2. Your business generates revenue before the intro period ends
3. You aggressively pay down balances before rates jump

## 4. Can You Service $150K in Minimums on $7K/Month?

| Income | Monthly Minimums (at 20% APR) | Surplus | Can Service? |
|--------|-------------------------------|---------|-------------|
| $7K salary from credit | $5,500 (on $150K) | $1,500 | **Barely** — and you're paying interest with borrowed money |
| $7K salary + $5K business revenue | $5,500 | $6,500 | **Yes** — but only if business revenue materializes |
| $7K salary, no business revenue | $5,500 | $1,500 | **No** — you're in a death spiral, paying interest with interest |

## 5. Legal and Tax Implications

### Is Paying Yourself a Salary from Credit a Legitimate Business Expense?

**Yes, with caveats:**

- **Single-member LLC (default taxation):** You take owner's draws, not salary. Draws are not a deductible business expense but are legitimate transfers from business to personal.
- **S-Corp elected LLC:** You must pay yourself a "reasonable salary" — the IRS monitors this.
- **Using credit for draws is legal** — the IRS cares about how you classify the income, not where the money comes from.
- **The credit card debt itself is not tax-deductible** as a business expense (interest on credit card debt CAN be deductible if used for business purposes).

### Where's the Legal Line?

| Action | Legal? | Notes |
|--------|--------|-------|
| Applying for multiple business cards | **Yes** | Standard business practice |
| Using business credit for business expenses | **Yes** | This is what it's for |
| Taking owner draws from LLC | **Yes** | Standard LLC practice |
| Using business credit for personal expenses | **Gray** — legal but risky | Risks piercing the corporate veil |
| Misrepresenting business revenue on applications | **No — FRAUD** | Credit card fraud, federal offense |
| Applying with fake EINs or businesses | **No — FRAUD** | Wire fraud, identity fraud |
| Not intending to repay | **No — FRAUD** | This is the critical line |

### The Critical Distinction
If you legitimately form an LLC, apply for credit, use it for business operations (including owner draws), and intend to repay — **it's legal**. If you have no intention of repaying or misrepresent your business — **it's fraud**.

## 6. Credit Score Impact

### Short-Term (Months 1-6)
- Multiple hard inquiries: -25 to -50 points
- High utilization (if Chase/Cap One report): -30 to -50 points
- **Net impact: 750 → 650-700 range**

### Medium-Term (Months 6-12)
- Inquiries age off: +15 to +25 recovery
- On-time payments: +10 to +20
- **Net: 680-720 range**

### Long-Term (If You Pay Off)
- Available credit increases: +20 to +30
- Payment history strengthens: +10 to +20
- **Net: Back to 750+ or higher**

### Long-Term (If You Default)
- Collections, charge-offs: **-100 to -200 points**
- Bankruptcy: **Score drops to 500-550**
- Recovery: **7-10 years**

## 7. Risk Assessment Summary

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|------|-----------|--------|------------|
| Can't reach $250K in credit | High | Medium | Plan for $75-150K, not $250K |
| Interest kills you after intro period | **Very High** | **Very High** | Must have revenue before intro expires |
| Business fails, personal guarantee triggered | Medium | **Very High** | Only stack what you can lose; maintain emergency fund |
| Credit score destroyed | Medium | High | Use Amex (doesn't report business balances) |
