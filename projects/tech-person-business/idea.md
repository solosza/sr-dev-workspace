# Idea: "I'm the tech person for people and small businesses that don't have one."

## Umbrella (how it is actually pitched)
I help people and small businesses solve everyday problems with technology, without
them having to deal with the tech. You describe the problem in plain words, I hand
back something that just works. The deliverable is whatever the problem needs (a
simple app, a form, a tracker, a scheduler, an automation), not a fixed product.

## What is actually sold
Not "an app" and not "AI." The customer buys "my annoying manual process now just
works and I did not have to touch anything technical." The build is maybe 20 percent
of the job. The other 80 percent (accounts, database, hosting, deployment, testing,
the technical plumbing) is exactly where a non-technical person hits a wall. That gap
is the product.

## Proof point (already happened, unpaid, for a friend)
A home-care nurse needed a shared visit sign-up sheet for her team. In one afternoon
I delivered a live tool (month calendar, tap a day, claim visits, saves instantly for
everyone, no double-booking), hosted for free, backed by a real database. She could
never have built or wired any of that herself.

## Why now
AI collapses the build time and cost enough that it is worth doing a bespoke tool for
a single nurse or a single small shop, work that used to need a dev shop, weeks, and
thousands of dollars.

## Customer
Non-technical individuals, sole proprietors, and small teams (clinics, trades, clubs,
offices, churches) with a specific manual process and no in-house tech.

## Distribution (the core of the model)
Solo, bootstrapped, referral-only. Zero marketing spend. Each satisfied non-technical
customer eagerly refers people in their own circle, because they look good for knowing
"a tech person" and the trust transfers with the intro. Every finished tool is a live
demo the next person sees. Referrals cluster by world (a nurse knows other nurses with
the same problem), so yesterday's build becomes today's template. Friend refers friend
refers friend.

## Rough economics (unvalidated)
One-time build fee per tool, optionally a small monthly for hosting, upkeep, and
changes. Repeatable because each niche's tool templatizes.

## Known tensions to pressure-test
- I am the bottleneck: referrals grow demand faster than one person grows supply.
  Word-of-mouth scales the asking, not me.
- Referral chains decay: realistically 0-2 referrals per happy customer, usually
  linear not viral unless actively engineered.
- Custom-forever vs productize: does niche clustering actually let me turn one-off
  builds into a few repeatable templates, or does every job stay bespoke (a
  time-capped services trap)?
- Willingness to pay: will these buyers pay real money, and how much, when free tools
  (Google Forms, no-code) exist?
- The escape hatch to test: referrals pool inside a niche, so the tool built for one
  nurse resells to the next five with small tweaks. That is the move from capped
  services to uncapped product, and it is referral-fed so still zero marketing.
