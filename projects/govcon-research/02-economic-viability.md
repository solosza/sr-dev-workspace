# Economic Viability — Government Contracting Subcontract Model

## Research Date
2026-05-28

## Executive Summary

Government contracting for small businesses is economically viable but requires patience. Margins of 8-20% are typical for services, with specialty AI/software niches at the higher end. The sub-$350K simplified acquisition space offers the best entry point with 5-20 bidders per opportunity. Expect 6-12 months to first award and 12-24 months to consistent revenue.

---

## 1. Typical Margins

### Industry Averages (2024 Deltek Clarity Report)

| Business Size | Average Profit Margin |
|--------------|----------------------|
| Small business | 8% |
| Medium-sized contractor | 20% |
| Large contractor | 24% |

### Margins by Contract Type

| Contract Type | Typical Margin |
|--------------|---------------|
| Cost-plus (cost reimbursement + fee) | 7-8% |
| Fixed-price | 10-13% |
| Time & Materials (T&M) | 8-12% |

### Specialty/Technical Services Premium
Contractors performing **specialty and technical services** (security clearances, rare technical skills, proprietary technology) earn the **highest profit margins** because limited competition allows premium pricing.

**AI/software consulting** falls into this specialty category — proprietary AI tooling and kernel-based automation are differentiated offerings with limited competition.

### Realistic Spread for the Subcontract Model
If you win a $200K services contract and subcontract 50% ($100K) to a similarly situated entity:
- **Your cost:** $100K (sub) + your labor (project management, technical direction)
- **Your revenue:** $200K
- **Gross margin on the subcontracted portion:** Whatever spread you negotiate above the sub's rate
- **Net margin (realistic):** 10-15% ($20K-$30K per contract)

## 2. Contract Types — Which Favor the Model

### Best for New Small Businesses

| Contract Type | Favorability | Why |
|--------------|-------------|-----|
| **Fixed-Price** | Best | You know the price upfront, can negotiate sub rates below your bid, clear margin |
| **T&M** | Good | Hourly rates are preset, you bill at your rate and pay subs at theirs, spread is the margin |
| **IDIQ** | Good (long-term) | Win once, receive task orders over years — but minimum order not guaranteed |
| **Cost-Plus** | Worst | Government audits costs, profit is a negotiated fee (7-8%), least room for spread |

### Fixed-Price Is the Sweet Spot
For the subcontract model, **firm-fixed-price (FFP)** contracts are ideal:
- You bid a price that includes your margin
- You negotiate sub rates independently
- The government doesn't audit your costs
- Your profit = bid price - (sub cost + your labor + overhead)

### IDIQ as a Growth Vehicle
IDIQ contracts provide a **pipeline** of task orders once won:
- Win the contract vehicle once
- Receive individual task orders as they arise
- Provides revenue stability over 3-5 year base + option periods
- Multiple-award IDIQs mean you compete for task orders against a small pool (typically 3-10 awardees)

## 3. Volume — How Many Opportunities Exist

### Federal Small Business Contracting Overall
- In 2024, the federal government awarded **over $150 billion** to small businesses
- The government's small business contracting goal is **23%** of total procurement dollars
- The sub-$350K simplified acquisition range is **automatically set aside for small business**

### IT/Software Services Volume
While exact monthly counts for sub-$250K IT contracts aren't published in aggregate, the opportunity pipeline is substantial:
- **SAM.gov** lists thousands of active opportunities at any given time
- IT services (NAICS 541511, 541512, 541519) are among the most frequently solicited categories
- AI/ML services are a growing subcategory with increasing federal demand

### Fiscal Year End Spending
Federal agencies must spend their budgets by September 30 (end of fiscal year). **Q4 (July-September)** sees a massive spike in contract awards, especially for sub-SAT purchases. This is the "use it or lose it" period where agencies push out remaining funds.

## 4. Competition Levels

### Set-Aside vs. Unrestricted

| Competition Type | Typical Bidders |
|-----------------|----------------|
| Unrestricted (full & open) | 50-100 |
| Small business set-aside | 5-20 |
| Specialty set-aside (8(a), HUBZone, SDVOSB) | 3-10 |

Set-asides reduce competition by **40-60%** compared to unrestricted competitions.

### The "Rule of Two"
Contracting officers must set aside contracts for small business competition if at least **two qualified small businesses** can perform the work at a fair price. This is the minimum threshold — actual competition is typically higher.

## 5. Payment Timeline

### From Bid to Cash

| Phase | Duration |
|-------|----------|
| Registration (SAM.gov, certifications) | 2-6 weeks |
| Opportunity identification + proposal prep | 2-4 weeks per opportunity |
| Government evaluation period | 30-90 days |
| Contract award to start of work | 1-4 weeks |
| Work performance to invoice | Varies (monthly billing typical) |
| Invoice to payment | **30 days** (Prompt Payment Act) |

### Total Time: Registration to First Payment
**Realistic:** 6-12 months from SAM.gov registration to first payment
**Optimistic:** 3-6 months (if circumstances align)

### Cash Flow Implications
- You must **fund operations** for 6-12 months before federal revenue arrives
- Maintain commercial revenue streams during the ramp-up
- Consider SBA loans or lines of credit for working capital

## 7. Verdict: Economic Viability

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Are margins worth it? | **Yes** — 10-15% on specialty AI/software is competitive with commercial consulting |
| Is the volume there? | **Yes** — $150B+ awarded to small businesses annually, IT is a top category |
| Is competition manageable? | **Yes** — set-asides limit to 5-20 bidders, specialty further narrows |
| Can you survive to first payment? | **Only if you maintain other revenue** — 6-12 month ramp is real |
| Best contract type? | **Fixed-price** for margin clarity, **IDIQ** for pipeline stability |

### Bottom Line
Government contracting is a **medium-term revenue play**, not a quick-cash model. Year 1 is about positioning, winning 1-2 contracts, and building past performance. Years 2-3 are where compounding past performance + IDIQ vehicles create a reliable revenue stream.
