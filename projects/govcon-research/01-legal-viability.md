# Legal Viability — Government Contracting Subcontract Model

## Research Date
2026-05-28

## Executive Summary

The "bid on SAM.gov, win contract, subcontract the work, capture the spread" model is **legally viable but constrained**. The primary constraint is FAR 52.219-14 (Limitations on Subcontracting), which requires the prime contractor to perform at least 50% of the work on service contracts. However, the "similarly situated entity" exception and mentor-protege/joint venture structures provide legal workarounds.

---

## 1. FAR 52.219-14 — Limitations on Subcontracting (The 50% Rule)

### What It Says
For **services contracts** (which is what AI/software consulting falls under), the prime contractor **cannot pay more than 50% of the contract amount to subcontractors** that are not "similarly situated entities."

| Contract Type | Max Subcontract % (to non-similarly situated) |
|--------------|----------------------------------------------|
| Services | 50% |
| Supplies | 50% (excluding materials cost) |
| General Construction | 85% |
| Specialty Trade Construction | 75% |

### Compliance Window
Compliance is measured **by the end of the base term** of the contract, then by the end of each subsequent option period. This is NOT per-invoice — it's cumulative over the performance period.

### What This Means for the Subcontract Model
You **cannot** simply win a contract and subcontract 100% of it. You must perform at least 50% of the work yourself (measured by cost of personnel). This fundamentally limits the pure "broker" model.

### The "Similarly Situated Entity" Exception

A **similarly situated entity** is a first-tier subcontractor that:
1. Has the **same small business program status** as what qualified the prime for the award
2. Is **small under the NAICS size standard** assigned to the subcontract

Work performed by similarly situated entities **counts toward the prime's self-performance requirement** — it does NOT count against the 50% subcontracting limit.

**Practical implication:** If you win a small business set-aside and subcontract to another qualifying small business, that work counts as YOUR work for the 50% rule.

**Limitation:** The similarly situated entity must **self-perform** the work with its own employees. If it further subcontracts to a non-qualifying entity, that work counts against the prime's 50% limit.

## 2. Small Business Set-Asides

### Simplified Acquisition Threshold (SAT)
- **2026 SAT:** $350,000 (effective October 1, 2025)
- **Micro-purchase threshold:** $15,000

### Automatic Set-Aside Rule
**All acquisitions between $15,000 and $350,000 are automatically set aside for small businesses** unless the contracting officer determines there aren't two or more responsible small business bidders.

For acquisitions **above** the SAT, contracting officers **shall** set aside for small business when there's reasonable expectation of competitive offers from at least two small businesses.

### Set-Aside Categories
- Small Business (SB)
- 8(a) Business Development
- HUBZone
- Service-Disabled Veteran-Owned Small Business (SDVOSB)
- Women-Owned Small Business (WOSB)
- Economically Disadvantaged Women-Owned Small Business (EDWOSB)

### What This Means
The sub-$350K space is **heavily tilted toward small businesses by law**. A new small business with SAM.gov registration competes in a smaller pool, not against large defense contractors.

## 3. Past Performance Requirements

### New Businesses Are Not Excluded
FAR does **not** require past performance for all contracts. The evaluation emphasis varies:

| Dollar Range | Past Performance Emphasis |
|-------------|--------------------------|
| Under $15K (micro-purchase) | Minimal — often awarded via purchase card |
| $15K–$350K (simplified acquisition) | Focus on price/technical capability, less on past performance |
| Above $350K | Past performance evaluated but "neutral" rating allowed for new businesses |
| Set-asides | Less emphasis on past performance |

### "Neutral" Rating for New Entrants
Agencies **cannot** penalize a business for having no past performance. A lack of past performance record is evaluated as **neutral** — neither positive nor negative.

### What Counts as Past Performance
- Federal contracts (prime or sub)
- **Commercial work** — private sector work similar in scope counts
- **First-tier subcontract work** — SBA now requires agencies to consider past performance obtained as a first-tier subcontractor
- Joint venture performance

### Building Past Performance
The recommended path for new entrants:
1. Start as a **subcontractor** to build federal experience
2. Use **commercial past performance** (AI/software consulting for private clients)
3. Target **simplified acquisitions** (<$350K) where past performance is less weighted
4. Consider **mentor-protege joint ventures**

## 4. Mentor-Protege Program & Joint Ventures

### SBA Mentor-Protege Program
- Pairs an experienced company (mentor) with a small business (protege)
- **Joint venture qualifies as small** for any small business contract if the protege individually qualifies
- Agreement lasts up to **6 years**
- Mentor provides: business development, accounting, HR, strategic planning, contracting guidance

### 2025 Rule Change
As of January 16, 2025, agencies **cannot** require proteges in mentor-protege joint ventures to demonstrate the same level of past performance as other offerors. This is a significant advantage for new businesses.

### Relevance to the Subcontract Model
A mentor-protege JV is a **legitimate alternative** to the pure subcontract model:
- The mentor handles the work (they have the expertise)
- The protege brings the small business status
- The JV qualifies as small
- Past performance requirements are relaxed

## 5. Legal Risks

### Pass-Through / Front Company Risk
The SBA actively investigates "pass-through" arrangements where a small business wins a set-aside contract and subcontracts most or all of the work to a large business. This is a violation of FAR 52.219-14 and can result in:
- Contract termination
- Suspension/debarment
- False Claims Act liability
- Loss of small business status

### Ostensible Subcontractor Rule
If a subcontractor performs the **primary and vital** requirements of the contract, the SBA may find that the subcontractor — not the prime — is the actual contractor. This makes the arrangement ineligible for small business set-asides.

### Mitigation
- Ensure the prime (you) genuinely controls the contract: manages delivery, provides technical direction, handles client communication
- Keep subcontracting within the 50% limit (or use similarly situated entities)
- Document the management/oversight role clearly
- Build genuine internal capability over time

---

## 6. Verdict: Legal Viability

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Is the pure broker model legal? | **No** — violates FAR 52.219-14 if you subcontract >50% to non-similarly situated entities |
| Is a modified model legal? | **Yes** — if you perform 50%+ of work yourself or subcontract to similarly situated entities |
| Can you start with no past performance? | **Yes** — neutral rating for new businesses, simplified acquisitions emphasize price/capability |
| Is mentor-protege a viable alternative? | **Yes** — joint ventures qualify as small, relaxed past performance requirements |
| What kills the pure broker model? | FAR 52.219-14 (50% rule) and the Ostensible Subcontractor Rule |

### Legal Model That Works
1. **Win contract as small business prime**
2. **Perform 50%+ of the work yourself** (project management, technical direction, deliverable review, client communication)
3. **Subcontract specialized execution** to similarly situated small businesses (counts toward your 50%)
4. **OR** form a mentor-protege JV where the mentor handles execution and you grow capability
