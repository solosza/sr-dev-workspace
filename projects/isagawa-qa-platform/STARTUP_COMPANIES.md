  Outreach Strategy

  1. Lead with the Trojan Horse Demo — it's perfect for YC companies

  YC startups are:
  - Moving fast with small teams (5-15 engineers)
  - Accumulating QA debt they know about but can't address
  - Cash-conscious but willing to pay for real ROI
  - Skeptical of sales pitches, receptive to proof

  Your free 60-min demo on their actual site is the ideal hook. They keep the code either   
  way. That's a no-brainer for a resource-strapped startup.

  2. Channel: LinkedIn + warm intros via YC network

  Cold outreach template (LinkedIn DM to CTO/founding engineer):

  Hey [Name] — saw [Company] in the YC S25 batch. Congrats.

  Quick question: how are you handling test automation as you ship faster? We run a free    
  60-min session where our AI generates working Selenium tests on your actual product — you 
  keep the code whether we work together or not.

  No pitch deck, no call — just tests that run. Worth 60 minutes?

  Why this works:
  - Acknowledges their YC status (flattering but not sycophantic)
  - Leads with their pain (shipping fast = no QA)
  - Zero risk offer (keep the code)
  - No ask for a "call" — the demo IS the call

  3. Batch outreach in priority order

  Week 1-2: Tier 1 companies (12 targets). Personalize each message — reference their       
  specific product and what flows you'd test.

  Week 3-4: Tier 2 companies (12 targets). Same approach.

  Parallel: Write a LinkedIn post like:
  "We ran test automation demos for 5 YC S25 companies last month. Here's what we found:    
  [insight about common QA gaps in early-stage startups]"

  This creates social proof and inbound from the batch.

  4. Find the right person

  For each company, target in this order:
  1. CTO / Co-founder (technical) — decision maker
  2. First engineering hire — often owns QA by default
  3. Head of Product — feels the pain of bugs in production

  Use LinkedIn, the YC directory, and their company websites to find names.

  5. Competitive intelligence

  Study Docket, QualGent, Propolis, and Bluejay closely. They're in the same batch, likely  
  networking with the same companies. Know their pitch so you can differentiate:
  - Your edge: open-source, human-in-the-loop, code they own vs black-box SaaS
  - Your edge: Screenplay architecture = maintainable tests, not throwaway scripts
