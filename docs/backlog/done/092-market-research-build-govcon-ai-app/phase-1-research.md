# Phase 1: Research — Validate the Government Contracting Model

## Status
NEW

## Location
`projects/govcon-research/`

## Objective
Determine whether the "bid on SAM.gov, subcontract the work, capture the spread" model is legally viable, economically attractive, and automatable.

## Research Questions

### Legal Viability
1. **FAR 52.219-14 (50% Rule):** When does this apply? What are the exact thresholds?
   - Service contracts: prime must perform 50% of personnel costs
   - Supply contracts: prime must perform 50% of manufacturing costs OR 50% of supply costs
   - Construction: prime must perform 15% of costs with own employees
   - Does this kill the "subcontract everything" model? Or only for certain contract types?
2. **Small Business Set-Asides:** What types exist? (8(a), HUBZone, WOSB, SDVOSB, total small business)
3. **Simplified Acquisition Threshold:** Is it $250K or $350K? What changes below it?
4. **Past Performance Requirements:** When is past performance required vs not required?
5. **"Similarly Situated" Subcontractors:** What does this mean for the 50% rule?

### Economic Viability
6. **Typical margins:** What spread can you realistically capture between government price and sub cost?
7. **Contract types:** Which types (fixed-price, time-and-materials, IDIQ) favor the broker model?
8. **Volume at entry level:** How many sub-$250K contracts are posted monthly on SAM.gov?
9. **Competition:** How many bidders typically compete for small set-aside contracts?

### Practical Viability
10. **LLC requirements:** What registrations are needed? (SAM, DUNS/UEI, NAICS codes)
11. **Bonding:** When is bonding required? What does it cost?
12. **Timeline:** Typical time from bid to award to payment?
13. **Risk:** What happens if your sub doesn't perform? Liability exposure?
