# Legal & Compliance — Trademark Risk, Platform Policies, Private Label Path

## Status
NEW

## Research Questions
- What is the legal exposure for importing and reselling replica goods in the US? (Lanham Act, Customs seizure rates, civil vs criminal thresholds?)
- At what volume does replica importing shift from low-risk personal import to prosecutable commercial infringement?
- Which platforms explicitly prohibit replica goods (Etsy, eBay, Depop, Poshmark, TikTok Shop)? What are the enforcement mechanisms and ban rates?
- What is the "inspired by" / private label legal defense — does it hold if logo/colorway is changed but silhouette and aesthetic are copied?
- How do successful streetwear brands launch without infringing — what's the private label / original brand path look like at small scale?
- Are there countries where replica importing has lower legal risk for the US-based reseller?

## What to Produce
- Risk spectrum table: replica (branded) → inspired-by → private label — legal exposure rating at each tier
- Platform policy matrix: which platforms allow/ban, enforcement risk, account protection strategies
- Private label alternative scoping: what it takes to build an "original" brand that captures the same aesthetic demand
- Go/no-go recommendation: which approach to take given risk tolerance

## Dependencies
- Market analysis (which platforms buyers are actually using)
- Sourcing research (whether suppliers will print branded vs blank)

## Notes
This is the gating document — sourcing and sales approach depend on the risk/compliance posture chosen here. Research this first before committing to a platform or supplier strategy.
